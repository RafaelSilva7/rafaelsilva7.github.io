My research concerns machine learning for time series, with a particular focus on learning useful representations from physiological signals when annotations are limited.

## Self-supervised learning and ECG foundation models

I study self-supervised learning for ECG classification, including cross-dataset learning, data-oriented training strategies, and adapting pretrained models to tasks with limited labeled data. This is the focus of my doctoral research at ICMC-USP, supervised by Diego Furtado Silva and supported by FAPESP.

Related work includes *Tackling Low-Resource ECG Classification with Self-supervised Learning*, *Exploring Data-Oriented Strategies for Training ECG Self-supervised Models*, and collaborative work on Chagas disease detection.

## Multivariate and multimodal time-series learning

My collaborative work explores how to combine information from multiple signal channels and contextual data. Examples include multimodal fusion for multivariate time-series classification and contextual language-informed cardiac pathology classification (CLIC).

## Time-series alignment and geophysical applications

During my master's research at UFSCar, I investigated partial automation of seismic-to-well ties using matching-region estimation, Dynamic Time Warping, and segmented global optimization. This work connects time-series alignment methods with geophysical interpretation.
